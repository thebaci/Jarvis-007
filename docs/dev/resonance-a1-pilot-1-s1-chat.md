# Warehouse goods receiving guide

This document is for new staff who have never received goods before. Work through steps 1 to 7 in order, without skipping a step or changing the order.

## What to prepare

- A pen to write on the delivery note.
- The warehouse ledger.
- An empty area to set damaged goods apart.

## Receiving steps

1. **Get the delivery note from the driver.** When the truck arrives, ask the driver for the delivery note. Do not sign anything on it yet.

2. **Count the packages as soon as you unload.** Count the total number of packages (boxes, sacks, parcels) unloaded from the truck and compare it with the number written on the delivery note. Do this **before letting the driver leave**. If the counts do not match, tell the driver right then.

3. **Inspect the outside of each box.** Any box that is **dented, wet or torn**:
   - Set it apart in the damaged goods area, never mixed with good goods.
   - Write on the delivery note that it is damaged (say which box and what kind of damage).

4. **Check each line of goods on the delivery note.** Go through the note line by line, and for each line check 2 things:
   - **Item code**: does the item actually received have the code written on the note?
   - **Quantity**: does the amount actually received match the amount written on the note?

   Mark any mismatched line on the note right away and write the actual quantity received beside it.

5. **Sign for receipt.** Sign only after steps 2, 3 and 4 are done.
   - Everything there, nothing damaged: sign normally.
   - Something short or damaged: **write clearly next to the signature** what is short, how many, and what is damaged. For example: "Short 2 boxes code A123. Damaged 1 box code B456 (wet)."

6. **Let the driver go.** Only after you have signed and made your notes.

7. **Enter it in the warehouse ledger the same day.** Record the **quantity actually received** (not the quantity on the note) in the warehouse ledger, on the day the goods arrive, not the next day.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Signing the note first and counting later | No complaint is possible for shortages or damage | Count, inspect and check first, then sign (step 5) |
| Letting the driver go before counting the packages | Nobody confirms a shortage of packages | Count the packages while the driver is still there (step 2) |
| Counting only the total packages, not checking each line | The box count is right but the code is wrong or goods inside are missing | Check the item code and quantity of every line (step 4) |
| Leaving dented, wet or torn boxes mixed with good goods | Damaged goods get sold or you cannot tell which batch they came from | Set them apart and note them on the delivery note (step 3) |
| Signing without writing anything next to the signature despite shortages or damage | Treated as received in full and in good condition | Write the shortage or damage clearly next to the signature (step 5) |
| Entering the ledger by the quantity on the note | The ledger does not match the real goods | Enter the quantity actually received (step 7) |
| Waiting until the next day to enter the ledger | Forgetting, wrong numbers | Enter the ledger the same day (step 7) |
